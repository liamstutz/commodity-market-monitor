### Week of 2026-09-21: Brent's war premium eases, but the spread is still extreme

**What happened:** Spot Brent fell about $16 to $114.89 and WTI fell about $11 to $96.41 (EIA, Sept 22). That narrowed the WTI–Brent spread from –$23.78 to –$18.48, which is still one of the widest levels of the past year. The 3-2-1 crack spread kept rising, to $73, near a one-year high, so refined products are still tight. US commercial crude stocks rose 3.0 million barrels to 426.4 million.

**My call:** I committed my first call: the WTI–Brent spread narrows by at least $1 by Oct 9. Most of the narrowing I expected happened before my call started counting, so it's graded from –$18.48. I still expect more narrowing. At this spread, exporting US crude is still very profitable, and reports of phased US–Iran talks on reopening Hormuz point toward a smaller war premium in Brent.

**What I'm watching:**

- Daily tanker traffic through Hormuz, compared with the recent average of about 17 ships a day.
- Any more attacks on Yanbu, Saudi Arabia's Red Sea export route.
- US crude exports in next Wednesday's EIA report. If exports aren't rising with a spread this wide, my export argument is wrong.
