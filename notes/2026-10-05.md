**What the evidence says about the thesis:** EIA reports crude exports rose 289,000 b/d to 3.570 million b/d in the week ended September 25. That is consistent with the export incentive I described. However, commercial crude stocks still rose 0.922 million barrels to 427.320 million, so the stronger claim that exports were already tightening aggregate US stocks has not been established. A wide benchmark spread alone does not establish export profit after freight, quality and transport costs. I am not treating the price move as proof of the earlier geopolitical explanation.

**New call: NY Harbor 3-2-1 crack spread up at least $2/bbl by October 16.** The [new call file](https://github.com/liamstutz/commodity-market-monitor/blob/main/calls/2026-10-05-crack-widens.yml) tests whether product tightness supports refining margins. Crude runs fell 554,000 b/d to 16.257 million b/d, with utilization at 92.5%. Gasoline stocks fell 1.7 million barrels and distillate stocks fell 2.3 million, even as crude inventories built. My inference is that lower throughput can support product prices relative to crude. This is a moderate-confidence forecast, not an observed outcome or a claim about market consensus.

The latest available dashboard crack is **$67.038/bbl on September 29**, down $6.078 from September 22. The bullish call therefore looks for a reversal of the latest weekly decline. The $69.038 level is only an illustrative target against today's available snapshot: the actual scoring baseline will be the last observation before the effective call date once delayed data arrive. The metric is the project's NY Harbor spot-product/WTI proxy, not a futures crack or a refiner's realized net profit.

**What would change my view:** A rebound in refinery runs alongside product inventory builds would weaken the supply argument. Crude rising faster than gasoline and diesel would also work against the call. I will watch the next EIA balance for those developments and keep the first call open until its scheduled resolution.
